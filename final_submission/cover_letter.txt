Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript, "Towards the Use of Simulated Environments to
Evaluate sEMG-Based Prosthetic Knee-Angle Regression," for publication as a
Research Article in BMC Biomedical Engineering.

The study addresses a practical limitation in prosthetic-regression research.
Knee-angle predictors are normally compared with offline error measures, even
though the intended use is physical control. We first confirm, in a separate
90-participant group, that surface electromyography adds predictive information
beyond knee-angle history at a 100-ms forecast horizon. We then place the paired
predictions into matched, stable level-walking simulations and measure the added
whole-body instability. The result is informative precisely because the two
tests disagree: the sEMG improvement is statistically reliable, but it does not
produce a detectable reduction in simulated instability. A seven-checkpoint
accuracy path further shows a marked deterioration only for the least accurate
model and identifies the interval in which a future, denser experiment should
look for a physical transition.

The work is suited to BMC Biomedical Engineering because it combines biomedical
signal processing, lower-limb prosthetic control, biomechanics, and a reusable
physics-based evaluation method. The manuscript includes participant-level
inference, an sEMG ablation whose success requirements were set before the
confirmation group was evaluated, a timing control, MoCapAct motion matching,
zero-error controller checks, all 560 planned numerical simulations,
actual simulation recordings, and machine-readable derived evidence.

This manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been
approved by the author. The author declares no competing interests. The study is
a secondary computational analysis of anonymized public data and recruited no
new participants. The relevant ethics approval and consent information from the
source dataset are reported in the manuscript.
